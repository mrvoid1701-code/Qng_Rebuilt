## The central OPEN challenge — manifold selection

The honest debt: a GENERIC random causet is **not** manifold-like. The Kleitman–Rothschild
result (the "entropy problem") shows that random posets DOMINATE the uniform
sum-over-causets, and these are non-manifold-like — three-layered, non-local, with NO
localized particles. So the particle sector requires a dynamical reason the sum favours
manifold-like orders. **This is OPEN, and shared by all of causal-set theory.**

## QNG 2.0's candidate mechanism (a conjecture, labelled as such)

QNG 2.0 has an ingredient QNG 1.0 and bare causal sets lack at this junction: **the matter
field is part of the action**. Conjecture — **matter as a manifold-selector**: the field
action `S_field[C,ψ] = Σ ψ*(B+m²)ψ` is well-behaved (local, finite, with the clean KG
spectrum of rung 0) ONLY on manifold-like causets; on non-manifold-like orders the BD
operator `B` is wildly non-local and `S_field` is large/ill-conditioned. Including matter
in the sum `Z = Σ_C ∫Dψ e^{i(S_grav+S_field)}` could therefore DYNAMICALLY SUPPRESS
non-manifold-like causets — turning the entropy problem's "bug" into a matter-driven
selection. This is a research direction, NOT a result; it must be tested (does adding
S_field measurably reweight the sum toward manifold-like orders?). Flagged CONJECTURE.
